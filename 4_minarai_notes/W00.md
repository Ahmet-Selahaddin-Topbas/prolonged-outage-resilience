## 1. Where my interest began
In my first year I saw how critical and how complex energy is. To study it at all, several disciplines have to be brought together. That suited my wish to make sense of complexity and to build systems. As a Management Information Systems student looking for a field to specialise in, I found one here. The step towards hydrogen came through a summer course on exergy and its applications in April 2026.

## 2. Why Léargas SI came first
In case studies and projects, being limited to a technical, an economic or a political view breaks the common ground between disciplines and leaves blind spots. I wanted one reference language for evaluation: technical, financial, political, ecological and social views, plus a lens for energy quality (exergy). Léargas SI came from that wish, and after the exergy course I added the exergetic lens.

## 6. What I got wrong or was surprised by
- I expected a source to be a paper and it was an annual report; I expected a link to be right and it pointed elsewhere. Both are in the Process Journal corrections.
- In the first model runs all options tie at short durations, so my prediction P1 ("best at short durations") cannot be tested as written. I will keep P1 as it is and add a revised prediction file instead of editing it.
- Expectation, not result: from reading, I expected hydrogen to show a clearer efficiency advantage over batteries. A battery returns most of the electricity, while the electricity-to-hydrogen-to-electricity chain loses much of it; against that stands the advantage of long-duration storage. So the two balance each other, and conditions must be judged carefully. This is what I understood from the literature, not a result of my model, which has no real data yet (see prediction P3).
