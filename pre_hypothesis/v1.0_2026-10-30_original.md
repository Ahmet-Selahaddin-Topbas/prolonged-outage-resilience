# Predictions (written before any model run)
*Dated 30 Oct 2026. No model has been run. These statements are recorded so that the results can later be compared with them, including where they turn out wrong. Edits after this date are added as dated corrections, never as silent changes.*

| # | Prediction | Basis | Would be wrong if |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | A diesel generator without refuelling is best at short durations and drops sharply once its on-site fuel is exhausted. | Fuel autonomy is a hard limit. | Its survivability falls gradually, or it is not best even at short durations. |
| P2 | A battery alone holds the critical tier (labs/servers) for hours, not days, in summer. | Cooling load in Adana summers is high. | It holds the tier for more than 24 h at plausible sizes. |
| P3 | The hydrogen chain gains an advantage only beyond a duration threshold and with sufficient sizing; the threshold rises in heat waves. | Round-trip losses and cooling load. | It is ahead at short durations, or the threshold does not shift in heat waves. |
| P4 | Outages starting at night are the worst starts for solar-based options. | No solar input until morning. | Night starts are not worse. |
| P5 | No single source has zero regret across all durations. | Each source has a different limit. | One source has the lowest regret everywhere. |
| P6 | Water supply for electrolysis is not a limiting factor. | At least about 9 kg of water per kg of hydrogen. | Water need exceeds a plausible campus supply. |

## What would change my mind
- A result in which hydrogen is never ahead under any tested condition: this is reported as a valid finding, not hidden.
- Evidence that real campus load differs strongly from the synthetic profile: results are then recomputed with the real load and the change is documented.
- A published work that offers the full combination described in the originality statement: the statement is revised and the revision is logged.
